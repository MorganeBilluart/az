When I’m around you, I feel like a kid. Curious, playful, but also calm and peaceful enough to rest. A kid, too, because I’m missing the words. They are forming in my mind and in my heart, but they are not yet fully shaped when they finally make their way out of my mouth. Even typing this here feels difficult. Not because I’ve suddenly forgotten how to write, but because the thought of you makes my senses melt, and I suddenly fall silent as memories pass through me. I don’t know how long it takes for an image to become imprinted as a memory, but since this summer, and especially since I’ve been back in France, these pictures have been taking shape with increasingly clear and sharp details in my mind. These days, my brain likes to re-render the first time I saw your face: the dimmed brown and yellow colours as you walked out of the bathroom, the not-so-epic cocktail made by Marie and you, sitting right beside me. I won’t enumerate all the images that now come to mind when I think about what we share (there are plenty). I would rather suggest that, if we are image collectors without protocols or a camera, we are doing an amazing job of travelling through vistas and conversations and somehow imprinting them. I would be lying if I said I instantly knew where this was going, or that I had a strong intuition about what our connection could mean. Instead, the slow and patient pace of getting to know each other, without rushing into ideas or conclusions, taught me that there is beauty in uncertainty, and a certain grace in the absence of a mould or a specific romantic projection. Looking back, I see a particular beauty in the way we both dedicated ourselves to each other with such natural ease and peace, without, I suppose, the pressure of things having to turn into diamonds and rings. There is, too, a sense of hope and gratitude that grows from any genuine and fruitful connection, especially at a time in history which, as we know and discuss, does not appear to be the brightest. With you around, almost everything becomes a subject of bright inquiry and playful curiosity. The world does not feel doomed. The future, while uncertain, seems navigable, with a few compasses in our hands: art, science, curiosity and, of course, community. Thank you for showing me a part of your world. A universe we often discussed, but then also got to experience with all of our senses. An experience that allows me to better understand where you come from and what you aspire to do or change. To navigate the world in each other’s presence is a gift of softness and endless interest. You’re the best conversationalist I’ve ever had the pleasure of spending time with on earth, and being around you has rewired many parts of my heart and brain. I already miss the way everyday things are experienced and reinterpreted with you. I hope we get to have (lots of) time, softness, and playfulness again. Morgane
